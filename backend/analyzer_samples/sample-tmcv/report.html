Executive Summary & Narrative

SUMMARY

This report examines Tata Motors Limited (TMCV.NS) between 01 January 2026 and 23 August 2026, covering 157 trading days. It is a structured case study, not investment advice, collecting what the Indian financial press published about the company over that period and measuring how the stock moved relative to ^NSEI, to identify days where notable coverage coincided with an unusual company-specific price move. Coincidence in time is not evidence that an article caused a price move.

Price moves are reported as abnormal returns: the company's actual return minus the benchmark's return that day. There was not enough price history before this window to fit a full market model, so the stock is assumed to move one-for-one with the index. For a stock that is more volatile than the market, this assumption will overstate the abnormal return.

How much weight to put on the numbers below: abnormal returns are standardised against this analysis window's own spread rather than a clean estimation window beforehand, which understates how unusual the largest moves in it really are.

Across 451 collected items of coverage, 6 candidate days met both tests. The most prominent was 13 August 2026, with an abnormal return of +4.05% alongside strongly positive coverage.

The top candidate's abnormal return sat 2.1 standard deviations from this stock's own typical daily variation. A permutation test — which does not depend on that z-score's normality assumption — checked this directly: across 172 random comparable-length windows drawn from this stock's own price history, 16.3% were at least as extreme (p = 0.163). It also held up under a range of nearby threshold choices, not just the exact ones configured: flagged in 9 of 9 combinations tried.

The other 151 trading days in the window did not clear both bars at once: 37 days had busy or strongly-toned coverage without a matching unusual price move; 10 days moved unusually without correspondingly unusual coverage; 4 days saw an unusually large price move with no coverage collected for it at all in this run's sources — a real gap, not a finding; 100 days were routine on both counts. Only a day that clears both — unusual coverage and an unusual price move — becomes a candidate; either alone is not enough on its own.

Of the 6 flagged days, 6 showed a price move in the direction the coverage's tone would suggest and 0 did not. Days where the two disagree are kept in the report rather than filtered out, because they are often the more interesting cases.
